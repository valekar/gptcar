# One cut, many windows

Premise: On New Year's Eve the beast Nian comes to town, and one girl's giant paper-cut lights up the village to scare it off.

Three structures considered:
1. A single object or gesture leads a continuous journey through the premise.
2. Three paired before/after plates reveal the change through the medium.
3. A workshop assembly shows the world built, then the world acting.

Choice: a subject-led journey with a material event at the turn. Its particular arc is: Nian appears over folded red village → girl cuts a small wedge → four then eight petals unfold to a huge window flower → all village windows repeat it and Nian retreats.

Independent benchmark: learn the readable silhouettes and motivated movement of wordless illustrated shorts, and the physical reveal order of the real craft. Do not borrow a reference image, design, camera route, melody or demo source. The exact native grammar is the assigned STYLE.md.

Shot and camera map: 0–5 low parallax beast; 5–10 tabletop scissors macro; 10–14 rotation around eightfold unfolding; 14–20 pull back to lit windows and departing beast.

Beat sheet: seconds 0–2 establish the concrete subject; 2–4 reveal context; 4–6 begin action; 6–8 follow; 8–10 accumulate detail; 10–11 hold; 11–12 anticipate; 12–14 material turn; 14–16 consequence; 16–18 widen; 18–20 final echo. The shot windows above take precedence for this film.

Cue map: original 90–120 BPM procedural miniature, synced to material and subject events; no voice. Use the style's instrument family as closely as supported by the shared synthesizer. The audio.json event list is the executable cue map. Breath windows 10.5–11.5 and 17–17.6 leave music and sustained effects quiet.

Sound design: opening = paper or scene ambience plus a solo motif; journey = matter-specific scratch/paper/wind cues plus short phrase; turn = isolated hit or brush gesture; ending = higher, resolved phrase, scene bed quieting. No looped soundtrack. Transitions use early material cues followed by the next section's note.

Type: sparse words placed in paper margins, cartouches, file labels or the physical object according to the style; never a generic end card. No narration or subtitle overlay. All imagery is original deterministic procedural code, with no external image assets.
