# The Little Rush

Three candidate structures:
1. A full rush-hour system pauses for one pedestrian.
2. Dawn to morning in one locked view.
3. Follow a red bus through the whole town.

Chosen: 1. This gives the subject a readable action, reversal and payoff in twenty seconds.

Logline: Morning cars, a commuter train and tiny people move through a bright town; time drops to real speed for one missed crossing.

Benchmark: learn material legibility, held performance and frame-size hierarchy from the style contract’s general historical craft references; take no specific characters, shot layouts, melodies or logos. Original scene code only; upstream demo sources were not read.

Shot, beat, camera and sound map:
- 0–5s: High oblique slow slide: dense stepped traffic crosses sunny streets. Marimba phase pattern.
- 5–10s: Top-down rotate: train and cars interlock at crossing; sharp band points to red bus.
- 10–15s: Closer high angle: time slows and one pedestrian waits; cars stop, crossing clears. Music pauses.
- 15–20s: High crane: pedestrian crosses, town resumes; train curls away. Percussion returns with a bell.

Cue grid: 120 BPM; cuts at the listed section boundaries, small foley on each action. Original marimba/pluck motif, bass response, deliberate rests before the turn and final payoff. Ambient wind or room tone is quiet under the score. Two short breathing gaps are written into the event schedule. No narration or subtitles; all text is a scene label or practical sign. The main story must read without text.

Look proof: render 2, 10 and 17 seconds with the deterministic still renderer, inspect imagery and repair failures. All camera/light/state functions seek directly from time.
