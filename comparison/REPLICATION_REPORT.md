# GPTcar: 51 reference-matched recreations

All **51 of 51** full-length recreations have passed independent scene/audio review, with a complete source-informed reconstruction brief, a new film source bundle and a render record tied to the accepted source/movie. This replaces the rejected first pass of abbreviated 20-second studies. The single-page gallery is ready for publication at [valekar.github.io/gptcar](https://valekar.github.io/gptcar/). This report does not announce a verified live deployment: the final strict release build, packaging and deployment verification remain release requirements.

## What this run compares

The left film is the original LemoLab / Claude Opus 5.5 production. The right is a GPT-6.1 Sol film, generated with High reasoning, following the original published story, scene order, narration and on-screen text, shot timing and production scope. The team examined every original sequence, its published STYLE/DEMO specifications and script/timing data, then wrote detailed reconstruction prompts before assembling new scenes. Regular reference sheets were supplemented with exact decoded frames for short shots, gestures, UI states and transitions.

**The original private conversation prompts were not published.** The canonical briefs distinguish verbatim published style instructions and script text from reconstructed shot descriptions. They are source-informed prompts, not recovered private chats or evidence that both models received identical original inputs.

This is a qualitative recreation exercise, not a blind or controlled model benchmark. The workers could inspect the original films, specifications, attributed reusable components and licensed source assets. There was no equal-budget, isolated-prompt experiment, automated model ranking or complete cost/time comparison. Acceptance means a close reconstruction with disclosed differences; it does not mean pixel identity, identical voice performance or an identical musical recording.

## Complete source and delivery scope

The [canonical brief index](matched-briefs/README.md) covers all 51 films. Each Markdown brief and adjacent JSON retain source paths/hashes, published script or narration, exact text inventories, timed scene coverage, camera/action/material requirements and asset provenance. Each new source bundle contains `PROMPT.md`, `TREATMENT.md`, renderable code, `narration.json`, `score.json`, `CREDITS` and `generation.json`; factual films also retain their evidence and limitations in `FACTS.md`. [Per-style replication guidance](../styles/README.md) is supplemented by `styles/<slug>/GPT_REPLICATION.md` and the [skill's replication mode](../.agents/skills/gptcar/references/replication.md).

The 51 render records total **89,949 video frames and 48 minutes 47.833 seconds**: 43 films at 24 fps, four at 30 fps and four at 60 fps. Their recorded frame counts, resolutions and frame rates match the corresponding measured original reference records. These are full original delivery lengths, with no common 20-second cap. Production canvases and native pixel/CRT/internal rasters are documented separately from the encoded delivery format. A container's reported duration can differ slightly from the exact frame-count/fps clock; the reference frame count and frame rate govern delivery.

## Authorship, assets and licences

New scene assembly, staging, camera/action reconstruction and animation were authored for the GPT versions. Substantial original components can remain: style/material engines, articulated character or action rigs, portrait/sprite/font assets, reusable UI/prop drawing, CAD geometry, photographs, calibrated imagery and datasets. They are itemized in each `CREDITS` and `generation.json` shared-component list. Such components and artwork retain their original authorship and licences; they are not presented as newly invented GPT artwork. The original STYLE.md files remain unchanged against the 51-file SHA-256 baseline pinned in `gptcar.json`.

Original scene-runtime replay, exported original frames and the original master soundtrack are not new GPT output. Reference screenshots are analytical material, not runtime artwork. Original film/story credits remain attributed, while new end credits identify the recreation honestly. MIT code does not relicense third-party photographs, fonts, sample libraries, models, data or event identities; per-film notices and factual caveats remain authoritative.

## Fresh speech, music and audio evidence

Narration and short commands use the published input text and voice direction, synthesized as new takes. Originals without narration remain without an added explanatory voiceover. Wren's typed dialogue, nonverbal vocalizations, crowd layers and other special cases follow their original conventions. Where a published command uses only the first word of a longer synthesis input, the new full input and independently located trim boundary are recorded rather than relabeling the input as a shorter take.

Scores and material-specific sound effects are newly composed, performed with licensed sample instruments or synthesized from the documented musical direction, tempo, section structure and cue times. Reused sample libraries and generic audio engines are credited. Original soundtrack recordings are not reused. Fresh arrangements, synthesis envelopes, instrumental voicings and voice cadence can differ from the originals, as the per-film notes disclose.

**Exact input text and ASR output are different evidence.** Script comparisons establish which words were supplied to synthesis. Timing/onset checks, speech recognition and decoded-mix loudness/peak analysis test the resulting takes and mix. ASR can change punctuation, number notation, spelling, homophones, proper names or very short/nonverbal syllables. Secondary recognition resolved some cases; remaining uncertainties and intentional trims are recorded per film. An exact synthesis input is not a guarantee of a perfect audible realization, and an ASR mismatch alone is not proof of a changed script. The records do not claim human listening sessions or native-speaker certification. Consult each review's `audio_evidence` and source `REVIEW.md` for the actual checks and limits. For example, Paper Lantern retains 25 fresh Mandarin inputs, with 14 literal base-model and 15 literal medium-model ASR matches; this is not a claim of 25 exact recognized takes. Game Show records 160 timed voice events from 40 unique raw input specifications and 117 literal native-ASR event matches. Repeated-event counts, unique synthesis inputs and literal recognition counts are reported separately; uncertain short syllables remain disclosed.

## Review and release records

Workers inspected full-sequence reference sheets and matched source stills. A separate root agent reviewed completed-movie pairs at common decoded frame times, covering the scene/action sequence and additional repair targets. This is recorded agent inspection of selected frames across every scene, not a claim that a human watched every encoded frame. Reviews caught and repaired missing props/actions, wrong camera scale or crop, early/late transitions, unreadable text, incorrect UI/count progression and material/light/palette mismatches. Remaining differences in contours, glyphs, drawing detail, rig gestures, particles, camera microtrajectory and audio performance are disclosed beside each pair.

The evidence has distinct roles:

| Record | What it establishes |
| --- | --- |
| `reference-metadata/<slug>.json` | Original movie URL/hash and measured resolution, frame rate and frame count. |
| `matched-briefs/<slug>.md` and `.json` | Published versus reconstructed prompt/script provenance and complete scene requirements. |
| `../replicas/<slug>/generation.json` | Generating model/effort, matched-brief hash, source readiness and shared components. Source readiness is not final acceptance. |
| `replica-results/<slug>.json` | Actual encoded movie/source/brief/poster hashes, delivery probe, requested review times and technical/audio measurements. A render record is not visual acceptance. |
| `replica-reviews/<slug>.json` | Independent scene/audio verdict tied to the reviewed source and movie hashes, actual review coverage, audio evidence and disclosed differences. |

All **51 current films** now have accepted source-bound reviews. Final publication also requires a successful strict release build. The strict builder rejects a missing/changed movie or poster, stale source or brief, wrong model/effort, incorrect original delivery format or incomplete/mismatched review. Only a successful final gate establishes the release inventory; a source-ready flag or this documentation does not substitute for it. The initial rejected studies and their [historical report](REPORT.md) remain separate from the accepted v2 selection.

The publication layout is one page, with every original on the left and its GPT recreation on the right. Search, category filters and style hash links operate within that page. Pages serves the page and posters; full-quality MP4s are distributed through the repository's `comparison-v2` GitHub Release. Media and poster URLs carry content versions, and release files are verified against the recorded SHA-256 values. Final live-URL verification is performed by the publisher after deployment.
